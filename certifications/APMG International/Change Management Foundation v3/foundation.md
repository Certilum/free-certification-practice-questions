### **Planning and Measuring Change**

### 20. What is the primary focus of a change management plan, as distinct from a project management plan?

- [ ] **A)** Managing the human transition and cultural aspects of the change
- [ ] **B)** Detailing the technical deployment steps and resource allocation
- [ ] **C)** Outlining a one-time announcement schedule for stakeholders
- [ ] **D)** Providing a fixed, unalterable timeline for all activities

<details>
<summary> 🔍 Reveal Answer</summary>
   
#### **✅ Correct Answer: A**
 
> 💡  **Explanation** 
> 
> A change management plan focuses on the people side of change – building awareness, desire, knowledge, ability, and reinforcement. It is distinct from a project plan which emphasises technical tasks and deliverables. Options about technical steps, one-time announcements, or static timelines are incorrect because they either belong to project management or misrepresent the adaptive nature of change planning.
 
 
</details>

### 21. Which two of the following are leading indicators that help predict the success of a change initiative?

- [ ] **A)** Percentage of employees who have completed change awareness sessions
- [ ] **B)** Feedback from early adopters on the new process
- [ ] **C)** Quarterly revenue increase after implementation
- [ ] **D)** Reduction in operational costs six months post-change

<details>
<summary> 🔍 Reveal Answer</summary>
   
#### **✅ Correct Answer: A, B**
 
> 💡  **Explanation** 
> 
> Leading indicators such as training attendance and early adopter feedback predict future success and can be acted upon promptly. Lagging indicators like revenue growth and cost reduction confirm outcomes after the change is embedded; they are not predictive and cannot be adjusted early.
 
 
</details>

### 22. Given the following Python code that calculates a key metric, what does it return?

```python
def calculate_metric(unique_users, total_users):
    if total_users == 0:
        return 0
    return (unique_users / total_users) * 100

# Example usage
unique = 450
total = 600
result = calculate_metric(unique, total)
print(result)  # Output: 75.0
```

- [ ] **A)** Training completion rate
- [ ] **B)** User adoption rate
- [ ] **C)** Stakeholder satisfaction score
- [ ] **D)** Process compliance percentage

<details>
<summary> 🔍 Reveal Answer</summary>
   
#### **✅ Correct Answer: B**
 
> 💡  **Explanation** 
> 
> The code calculates adoption rate as the number of unique users who logged into the new system divided by the total number of target users, multiplied by 100. This is a common adoption metric, not training completion or satisfaction.
 
 
</details>
